Lotka-Volterra Predator-Prey Model for Steller Sea Lions and Their Fish Prey

This interactive Lotka-Volterra model explores how Steller sea lions interact with walleye pollock and Atka mackerel as fish prey.

Parameters

Adjust the parameters to examine population changes, phase dynamics, equilibria, and stability conditions.

Model

Variable key: x = walleye pollock, y = Atka mackerel, z = Steller sea lion.

dxdt = rxx(1 − xkx − αykx) − γxz − hxx
dydt = ryy(1 − yky − βxky) − δyz − hyy
dzdt = −γz + ζγxz + ηδyz − hzz

Ecological Interpretation

Let x(t) represent the population density or biomass of walleye pollock, y(t) represent the population density or biomass of Atka mackerel, and z(t) represent the population density of Steller sea lions. In this model, Steller sea lions are the predator, while walleye pollock and Atka mackerel are prey species that may also compete with one another for food and resources.

Parameters in the Pollock Equation

dxdt = rxx(1 − xkx − αykx) − γxz − hxx
Parameter Meaning
rxIntrinsic growth rate of walleye pollock
kxCarrying capacity of walleye pollock
αCompetition coefficient measuring the effect of Atka mackerel on pollock
γPredation rate of Steller sea lions on pollock
hxHarvesting rate of walleye pollock

The logistic term describes natural population growth limited by environmental carrying capacity. The competition term represents resource competition between pollock and Atka mackerel. The predation term models pollock consumption by sea lions, and the harvesting term represents harvesting pressure on pollock.

Parameters in the Atka mackerel Equation

dydt = ryy(1 − yky − βxky) − δyz − hyy
Parameter Meaning
ryIntrinsic growth rate of Atka mackerel
kyCarrying capacity of Atka mackerel
βCompetition coefficient measuring the effect of pollock on Atka mackerel
δPredation rate of sea lions on Atka mackerel
hyHarvesting rate of Atka mackerel

Atka mackerel grows logistically under environmental limitation. Pollock competes with Atka mackerel for prey or resources, sea lions consume Atka mackerel at rate δyz, and harvesting affects Atka mackerel at rate hyy.

Parameters in the Steller Sea Lion Equation

dzdt = −γz + ζγxz + ηδyz − hzz
Parameter Meaning
ζBiomass conversion efficiency from pollock consumption into sea lion population growth
ηBiomass conversion efficiency from Atka mackerel consumption into sea lion population growth
hzHarvesting rate of Steller sea lions

The term −γz represents natural mortality or energetic loss in sea lions. The terms ζγxz and ηδyz represent predator growth supported by pollock and Atka mackerel consumption. The term −hzz represents harvesting pressure on Steller sea lions.

Interpretation of Interaction Types

If α and β are positive, the two fish species compete for food or resources, with larger values implying stronger competition. Sea lions benefit from consuming both pollock and Atka mackerel, while both fish populations decrease due to predation. The harvesting parameters hx, hy, and hz model harvesting pressure, and large harvesting values may destabilize coexistence equilibria.

Equilibria and Routh-Hurwitz Stability

For each equilibrium, the Jacobian characteristic polynomial is written as λ3 + a1λ2 + a2λ + a3. Local asymptotic stability requires: a1 > 0, a2 > 0, a3 > 0, and a1a2 > a3.

Equilibrium Calculation

An equilibrium point (x*, y*, z*) occurs when all population rates are zero:

dxdt = 0, dydt = 0, dzdt = 0

The app tests feasible combinations where one or more species may be absent, solves the remaining linear equilibrium conditions, rejects negative or invalid populations, and keeps only biologically feasible equilibria.

Jacobian Matrix

Stability is evaluated by linearizing the system around each equilibrium. If F(x, y, z), G(x, y, z), and H(x, y, z) are the right-hand sides of the three equations, the Jacobian is:

J = ∂F∂x ∂F∂y ∂F∂z ∂G∂x ∂G∂y ∂G∂z ∂H∂x ∂H∂y ∂H∂z

This matrix measures how small changes in pollock, Atka mackerel, and sea lion biomass affect the short-term change rates near the equilibrium.
