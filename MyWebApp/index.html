Health Measures for New Mexico

This interactive web map presents selected CDC PLACES health measures across New Mexico census tracts. Users can explore spatial patterns in health outcomes, preventive care, risk behaviors, disability, health status, and health-related social needs across communities.

The maps use crude prevalence estimates from CDC PLACES data and link them to New Mexico census-tract boundaries. Popups provide tract-level values, population information, and 95% confidence intervals where available.

About the project

Health Measures for New Mexico is an interactive web mapping project that presents selected CDC PLACES health measures at the census-tract level across New Mexico. The map is designed to help users explore how health-related conditions and needs vary across different communities within the state.

The project focuses on several major categories of health indicators, including health outcomes, preventive care, health risk behaviors, disability, health status, and health-related social needs. By organizing the data into these categories, the web map allows users to examine both medical and social dimensions of health in a spatial format.

Users can select different indicators from the dropdown menus to update each map. Each census tract is shaded according to the selected measure, allowing users to identify geographic patterns, compare areas, and observe where certain health conditions or needs appear more concentrated. Clicking on a census tract opens a popup with additional information, including the selected prevalence estimate, population information, and confidence intervals where available.

This project uses CDC PLACES data linked to New Mexico census-tract boundary data. CDC PLACES provides local-level estimates for chronic disease, prevention, health behaviors, disability, and related health measures. The census-tract geography makes it possible to examine variation within counties and across smaller communities rather than only at the state or county level.

The purpose of this project is educational and exploratory. It is intended to support visual analysis, public health learning, and community-level comparison. The map can help students, researchers, public health professionals, and community members better understand spatial differences in health measures across New Mexico.

This web map was adapted from the GeoAIR Lab web-mapping framework developed by Dr. Liping Yang. The original framework was modified for a CDC PLACES-focused analysis of New Mexico health measures while preserving the interactive mapping structure of the original lab project.

Map Features:

  • Interactive census-tract maps for multiple health measure categories
  • Dropdown menus for selecting different CDC PLACES indicators
  • Color-shaded maps showing tract-level variation in selected measures
  • Popups with tract-level values, population information, and confidence intervals
  • Two-location comparison tool for selected census tracts
  • Summary statistics for each selected measure
  • Searchable data table for reviewing selected indicators across tracts

Important Note:

The values shown in this project are estimates and should be interpreted as tools for exploration, comparison, and learning. They should not be treated as direct clinical diagnoses or individual-level health records. The map shows population-level patterns across geographic areas.

Land Acknowledgement

As we engage in work across the diverse landscapes of New Mexico, we recognize and honor the deep-rooted connection that Indigenous peoples have maintained with this land since time immemorial. New Mexico is home to 23 federally recognized Native Nations, including the Navajo Nation, the Jicarilla and Mescalero Apache Tribes, and 19 Pueblo nations: Acoma, Cochiti, Isleta, Jemez, Kewa, Laguna, Nambe, Ohkay Owingeh, Picuris, Pojoaque, Sandia, San Felipe, San Ildefonso, Santa Ana, Santa Clara, Taos, Tesuque, Zuni, and Zia. We also acknowledge the historical presence of the Fort Sill Apache Tribe, now located in Oklahoma, and the Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, now located in Texas, whose ancestral ties to New Mexico continue to be significant. These sovereign nations each hold their own government, traditions, languages, and cultural practices.

We acknowledge the vital role that the Indigenous peoples of New Mexico have played in stewarding and protecting these lands and waters. Their wisdom continues to guide and inspire our efforts in conservation, environmental stewardship, and geospatial work. In alignment with this respect, we are committed to elevating and supporting the essential voices of these Tribes and Pueblos in our ongoing projects. We also recognize that Indigenous perspectives on land boundaries may differ from the administrative lines used in our tools and maps, and we invite any Tribes or Pueblos to collaborate with us to ensure their lands are represented in a way that reflects their sovereignty and cultural values.

About this CDC PLACES NM lab version

This version adapts the original GeoAIR Lab web-mapping example to the CDC PLACES New Mexico dataset. The code keeps the original folder structure and local library links, while replacing the active map logic with seven CDC category rows.

Note: the CDC NM CSV includes the PLACES health-measure fields, but it does not include the ACS-derived Non-Medical Factors fields. The seventh row is therefore kept as a placeholder for future data expansion.
